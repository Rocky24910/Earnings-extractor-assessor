Voyager Reports Record Third Quarter 2026 Revenue, Bookings and Backlog
Revenue increases 124% year-over-year and 68% sequentially; Raises full-year 2026 revenue guidance to $285 million–$305 million
DENVER, November 2, 2026 /Business Wire/ -- Voyager Technologies, Inc. [NYSE: VOYG] (“Voyager” or the “Company”) today announced financial results for the third quarter 2026.
Financial Performance Highlights
•Record backlog of $742 million, increasing 293% year-over-year and 121% sequentially, including $347 million added due to the Astrobotic acquisition
•Record quarterly revenue of $89 million, increasing 124% year-over-year
•Record quarterly bookings of $116 million, increasing 135% year-over-year, resulting in a 1.3x Book-to-Bill ratio
•Strong financial position with $815.7 million in total liquidity at quarter-end, including $553.6 million in cash and cash equivalents
Business Highlights
•Announced Anduril strategic partnership to deliver advanced defense technologies
•Awarded multi-million dollar contract for agentic AI spectrum operations platform
•Secured $298 million NASA award through Astrobotic, expanding lunar backlog
•Completed Griffin-1 acceptance testing, advancing preparations for late-2026 launch
•Awarded multiple biopharmaceutical research mission management contracts to the International Space Station, reflecting growing commercial demand
•NASA released Commercial LEO Destination Phase 2 RFP, marking the next major milestone in the program
•Appointed General B. Chance Saltzman, U.S. Space Force (Ret.), as Chairman of Voyager’s Board of Advisors to provide strategic counsel across the Company’s space and defense efforts
“Voyager delivered another exceptional quarter, achieving record revenue, record bookings and record backlog, driven by accelerating demand for our technologies and exceptional execution,” said Dylan Taylor, Chairman and CEO of Voyager Technologies. “Our record backlog provides meaningful multi-year revenue visibility and raising our full-year guidance reflects this momentum.”
“Voyager has emerged as a differentiated technology platform at the convergence of defense, national security and space, investing ahead of demand to scale the capabilities and capacity our customers need,” continued Taylor. “Supported by a strong balance sheet, and growing customer commitments, we are well positioned to deliver sustained growth and substantial shareholder value.”


Business and Financial Performance Results
Record bookings and backlog strengthen revenue visibility
Voyager generated $116 million in bookings during the third quarter – the strongest bookings quarter in Company history. The result compares with $49 million in the third quarter of 2025, representing year-over-year growth of approximately 135%. Trailing twelve-month bookings reached $329 million.
The Company ended the third quarter with a record $742 million in backlog, compared with $336 million at June 30, 2026, and $266 million at December 31, 2025.
The combination of continued bookings momentum and a substantially expanded backlog, including contributions from the Astrobotic acquisition, strengthens Voyager's visibility into future revenue and supports its growth trajectory into 2027 and beyond.
Revenue growth accelerates achieving new record level
Voyager generated $89 million in net sales during the third quarter of 2026, a year-over-year increase of 124% and 68% sequentially from the second quarter. For the nine months ended September 30, 2026, net sales totaled $177 million, an increase of 48% year-over-year, reflecting increasing demand for defense and national security solutions and contributions from recent acquisitions, partially offset by the wind-down of lower-margin space service contracts.

Gross profit increases with revenue growth
Gross profit increased to $10.2 million in the third quarter of 2026, compared with $6.1 million in the prior-year period, reflecting higher revenue across the Company's expanding portfolio. Gross margin was 11.5%, compared with 15.4% in the prior-year period, primarily reflecting investments in manufacturing capacity and strategic programs to support customer demand across defense, national security and space markets.
Sequentially, gross margin improved by 310 basis points compared with the second quarter of 2026, reflecting the favorable impact of revenue growth driven operating leverage.
Voyager remains focused on improving manufacturing efficiency, program execution and operating leverage as revenue continues to scale.
Advancing Starlab's long-term opportunity
Voyager continued to advance the development and commercialization of Starlab, its next-generation commercial space station initiative. As of September 30, 2026, Starlab had achieved 39 milestones and generated $212.5 million in cumulative milestone cash receipts since inception, including $1.5 million during the third quarter. The program has approximately $700 million in signed commercial reservations, up from approximately $500 million reported last quarter.
In early October, NASA released its Commercial LEO Destination (CLD) Phase 2 Request for Proposals, with submissions due in December 2026 and an award anticipated in spring 2027. This milestone marks a significant advancement in NASA’s transition toward commercial


space stations and represents a potential catalyst for Starlab’s next phase of development, commercialization and long-term growth.
Strategic innovation investments support long-term growth
Voyager continues to invest in differentiated technologies that address evolving defense and space requirements, including AI, advanced propulsion, resilient space architectures and mission-critical electronics.
For the third quarter of 2026, innovation spend (including customer-funded development activities) was 36.9% of net sales, excluding Starlab, and 49.3% on a consolidated basis – see Table 5 for additional details.
These investments support the expansion of Voyager's technology portfolio and long-term growth opportunities, while management remains focused on balancing innovation with operating discipline and improved profitability as the business scales.
Financial results and continued investment
Voyager continues to invest in technology development, manufacturing capacity and strategic programs to support customer demand across defense, national security and space markets. For the third quarter of 2026, we reported a net loss attributable to Voyager Technologies, Inc. of $(55.7) million, or $(0.91) per share, and a non-GAAP adjusted loss of $(52.8) million , or $(0.87) per share. Non-GAAP Adjusted EBITDA loss was $(44.9) million.
The increase in operating losses primarily reflects increased investment in technology development, expanded operational capabilities and the integration of acquired businesses. Management remains focused on driving operating efficiencies and improving profitability as the Company scales.
Strong financial position
Voyager ended the quarter with $553.6 million in cash and cash equivalents and total liquidity of $815.7 million, including $262.1 million of available revolver capacity, against $841.1 million of convertible notes, net. Our financial position provides significant flexibility to fund strategic growth initiatives, support program execution and invest in innovation while maintaining disciplined capital management.
Business Outlook
Voyager is raising the lower end of its full-year 2026 revenue guidance by $10 million to a revised range of $285 million–$305 million, representing expected year-over-year growth of approximately 72%–84%. The increased outlook reflects strong third-quarter performance, continued bookings momentum and growing visibility from the Company's record backlog, reinforcing management's confidence in its growth trajectory.

Adjusted EBITDA
We consider Adjusted EBITDA to be a useful, supplemental, measure of our operating performance. We use Adjusted EBITDA to supplement GAAP measures in evaluating the performance of our business and the effectiveness of our strategies, to make budgeting decisions, make certain compensation decisions, and to compare our performance against that of our peer companies, many of which present similar non-GAAP financial measures.
In addition, we believe Adjusted EBITDA provides a useful measure for period-to-period comparisons of our business, as it removes the impact of our capital structure and other items not indicative of our core operating performance from operating results.
We define EBITDA as net loss attributable to Voyager Technologies, Inc. plus (less) finance and interest expense, provision for income tax expense (benefit), and depreciation and amortization. We define Adjusted EBITDA as EBITDA adjusted for stock-based compensation, business acquisition costs, restructuring charges, impairment losses, income (loss) attributable to noncontrolling interests, interest income, and other items we do not believe are indicative of our core operating performance, including incremental organizational costs attributable to our initial public offering, changes in the fair value of earnout liabilities, and foreign exchange gain/loss.
Free Cash Flow
We consider free cash flow to be a useful, supplemental measure of our ability to generate cash on a normalized basis. We use free cash flow to supplement GAAP measures in evaluating our flexibility to allocate capital and pursue opportunities that may enhance shareholder value and the effectiveness of our strategies, to make budgeting decisions and to compare our performance against that of our peer companies, many of which present similar non-GAAP financial measures.
We believe that while expenditures and dispositions of property and equipment will fluctuate on a period-to-period basis, we seek to ensure that we have adequate capital on hand to maintain ongoing operations and enable growth of the business. Additionally, free cash flow is of limited usefulness in that it does not represent residual cash flows available for discretionary expenditures due to the fact the measures do not deduct the payments required for debt service and other contractual obligations or payments.
We define free cash flow as the sum of our cash (used in) provided by operating activities less our net capital expenditures. The net capital expenditures of the Company are defined as the gross capital expenditures for the purchase of property and equipment less the grant funding we received in order to make such purchases. Based on the nature of government grants for purposes of funding capital expenditures on our Starlab program or other government funded capital expenditure programs, these grants are pass through for purposes of making capital expenditures as they are directly used to source funding on capital expenditures. Our


calculation of free cash flow may not be comparable to the calculation of similarly titled measures reported by other companies.
Adjusted Net Loss Per Share
We consider adjusted net loss per share to be a useful, supplemental measure of our operations on a per share basis adjusting for items that are considered either non-operational or significant infrequent expenses or that are sources of income that are not recurring to the business on a frequent basis. We define adjusted net loss per share as the net income/loss attributable to common stockholders adjusted for stock-based compensation, business acquisition costs, restructuring, deferred income tax (benefit) expense, and other items mainly related to financing expenses and other individually immaterial items divided by our diluted basis number of weighted average shares outstanding during the period. Since the adjustments made for presentational purposes do not impact the tax basis of the Company, the adjustments have been presented on a tax free basis.
Innovation Spend
We are focused on delivering innovative solutions to the defense, national security and space end markets, and research and development is at the core of our business. We believe innovation spend and innovation spend excluding Starlab provide our management and investors useful measures of our aggregate spend on research and development type activities in support of our customers’ needs and our future growth.
However, innovation spend is an operating metric, not a financial measure calculated or presented in accordance with GAAP, and companies in our industry may calculate innovation spend or similar operating metrics differently than we do. We define innovation spend as research and development costs associated with IRS Section 174 categorization, as well as spend on designated development programs. Development programs are defined as initiatives that, when developed, will expand the Company’s product offerings under a customer funded arrangement. Innovation spend is comprised of various costs recognized in cost of sales and research and development costs within the consolidated statements of operations, as well as certain costs capitalized within property and equipment, net on our consolidated balance sheets. We define innovation spend excluding Starlab as innovation spend, minus the portion of innovation spend attributable to Starlab Space Stations.
