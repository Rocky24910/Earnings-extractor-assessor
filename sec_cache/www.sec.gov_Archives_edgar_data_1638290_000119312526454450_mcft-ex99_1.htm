FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE

MasterCraft Boat Holdings, Inc. Reports Results for the First Three Months of Its Transition Period

VONORE, Tenn. – November 10, 2026 – MasterCraft Boat Holdings, Inc. (NASDAQ: MCFT) today announced financial results for the first three months of its transition period ended October 4, 2026.

The overview, commentary, and results provided herein relate to our continuing operations, which consists of our Performance and Wake, Leisure, and Recreation and Sport Fishing segments.

On May 15, 2026, the Company completed the merger with Marine Products (“Marine Products Transaction”). As a result, the Company recognized incremental net sales of $59.3 million within the Recreation and Sport Fishing segment during the first three months of the Transition Period (as defined below) compared to the first three months of fiscal 2026. Results for the three-month period ended October 4, 2026 were also impacted by acquisition and integration costs, including the change in fiscal year-end, associated with the Marine Products Transaction.

In connection with the merger, the Company announced a change in its fiscal year-end from June 30 to December 31, effective July 1, 2026, to conform to the fiscal year-end historically used by Marine Products. As a result, the Company expects to file a transition report covering the six-month period from July 1, 2026 through December 31, 2026 (the “Transition Period”). Thereafter, the Company’s reporting periods will be based on its new December 31 fiscal year-end.

Highlights:

 

▪
Net sales for the first three months of the Transition Period were $154.1 million, up $85.1 million, or 123.4%, from the prior-year period. Excluding the contribution from the Marine Products Transaction, net sales increased $25.8 million, or 37.4%, compared to the prior-year period.
▪
Integration and synergy efforts continue to advance to plan
▪
Income from continuing operations during the first three months of the Transition Period was $12.1 million, or $0.50 per diluted share, up from $3.7 million, or $0.22 per diluted share, in the prior-year period
▪
Adjusted Net Income, a non-GAAP measure, during the first three months of the Transition Period was $12.2 million, or $0.50 per diluted share, up from $4.7 million, or $0.29 per diluted share, in the prior-year period
▪
Adjusted EBITDA, a non-GAAP measure, during the first three months of the Transition Period was $20.1 million, up $13.1 million from the comparable prior-year period

 

 

▪
Share repurchases of $4.8 million during the first three months of the Transition Period, with $18.7 million remaining under the repurchase program

Brad Nelson, Chief Executive Officer, commented, “We delivered results above our expectations in the first quarter of the Transition Period, with total company net sales growing more than 120% year-over-year. Importantly, our legacy business net sales grew 37% and delivered Adjusted EBITDA growth of nearly 140%, reflecting the deliberate product, commercial, and operational actions we have taken across our business to drive premium mix, clean channels, and reduce costs.”

Nelson continued, “The success of the next-generation X-Series is a clear example of the strength of our product and portfolio strategy and our ability to translate a deep understanding of our consumers into products that resonate in the market. Combined with our ongoing cost, productivity, and quality initiatives, those actions are driving stronger earnings and margin performance.”

Three-Month Period Results

For the first three months of the Transition Period, MasterCraft Boat Holdings, Inc. reported consolidated net sales of $154.1 million, up $85.1 million from the prior-year period. The increase in net sales was primarily due to incremental net sales of $59.3 million generated in our Recreation and Sport Fishing segment as a result of the Marine Products Transaction, and higher unit volumes, favorable model mix, and increased prices in our Performance and Wake and Leisure segments.

Gross margin percentage decreased 120 basis points during the first three months of the Transition Period, compared to the prior-year period. The decrease was primarily the result of the inclusion of our Marine Products business, partially offset by increased net sales and favorable cost absorption in our Performance and Wake and Leisure segments.

Operating expenses increased $8.9 million for the first three months of the Transition Period, compared to the prior-year period, due to incremental operating expenses within the Recreation and Sport Fishing segment, as well as additional acquisition and integration costs, including the change in fiscal year-end, related to the Marine Products Transaction, and higher selling and marketing costs.

Income from continuing operations was $12.1 million for the first three months of the Transition Period, compared to $3.7 million in the prior-year period. Diluted income from continuing operations per share was $0.50, compared to diluted income per share of $0.22 for the prior-year period.

Adjusted Net income was $12.2 million for the first three months of the Transition Period, or $0.50 per diluted share, compared to $4.7 million, or $0.29 per diluted share, in the prior-year period.

Adjusted EBITDA was $20.1 million for the first three months of the Transition Period, compared to $7.0 million in the prior-year period. Adjusted EBITDA margin was 13.0%, up from 10.1% for the prior-year period.

 

 

See “Non-GAAP Measures” below for a reconciliation of Adjusted EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA margin, Adjusted Net Income, Adjusted Net Income per share, and Free Cash Flow, which we refer to collectively as the “Non-GAAP Measures”, to the most directly comparable financial measures presented in accordance with GAAP.

Outlook

Concluded Nelson, “This quarter reinforces three important drivers of our long-term earnings potential. First, commercial and operational execution across our business is translating into higher earnings. Second, our expanded brand portfolio and growing dealer network are creating new opportunities for growth across categories and end markets. And third, the investments we are making to capture synergies and leverage the scale and capabilities of the combined company provide meaningful opportunities that we believe will drive further value creation.”

The Company’s outlook is as follows:

 

•
For the Transition Period we expect consolidated net sales to be between $293 million and $297 million, with Adjusted EBITDA between $32 million and $34 million, and Adjusted Earnings per share between $0.75 and $0.80. We continue to expect capital expenditures to be approximately $9 million for the Transition Period.

Non-GAAP Measures

 

EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA, EBITDA margin, and Adjusted EBITDA margin

 

We define EBITDA as income from continuing operations, before interest, income taxes, depreciation and amortization. We define Adjusted EBITDA as EBITDA further adjusted to eliminate certain non-cash charges or other items that we do not consider to be indicative of our core and/or ongoing operations. For the periods presented herein, the adjustments include share-based compensation, senior leadership transition costs, Marine Products acquisition and integration costs, and inventory step-up. We define EBITDA margin and Adjusted EBITDA margin as EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA, respectively, each expressed as a percentage of Net sales.

 

Adjusted Net Income and Adjusted Net Income per share

 

We define Adjusted Net Income as income from continuing operations, adjusted to eliminate certain non-cash charges or other items that we do not consider to be indicative of our core and/or ongoing operations and reflecting income tax expense on adjusted net income before income taxes at our estimated annual effective tax rate. We define Adjusted Net Income per share as Adjusted Net Income divided by the weighted-average basic and diluted shares outstanding. For the periods presented herein, these adjustments include other intangible asset amortization, share-based compensation, senior leadership transition costs, Marine Products acquisition and integration costs, and inventory step-up.

Free Cash Flow

We define Free Cash Flow from continuing operations as net cash flows from operating activities less purchases of property, plant, and equipment.

 

 

 

The Non-GAAP Measures are not measures of net income, operating income, or net cash flows as determined under GAAP. The Non-GAAP Measures are not measures of performance in accordance with GAAP and should not be considered as an alternative to net income, net income per share, or net operating cash flows determined in accordance with GAAP. Additionally, Adjusted EBITDA is not intended to be a measure of cash flows. We believe that the inclusion of the Non-GAAP Measures is appropriate to provide additional information to investors because securities analysts and investors use the Non-GAAP Measures to assess our operating performance across periods on a consistent basis and to evaluate the relative risk of an investment in our securities. We use Adjusted Net Income and Adjusted Net Income per share to facilitate a comparison of our operating performance on a consistent basis from period to period that, when viewed in combination with our results prepared in accordance with GAAP, provides a more complete understanding of factors and trends affecting our business than does GAAP measures alone. We believe Adjusted Net Income and Adjusted Net Income per share assists our board of directors, management, investors, and other users of the financial statements in comparing our net income on a consistent basis from period to period because it removes certain non-cash items and other items that we do not consider to be indicative of our core and/or ongoing operations and reflecting income tax expense on adjusted net income before income taxes at our estimated annual effective tax rate. The Non-GAAP Measures have limitations as an analytical tool and should not be considered in isolation or as a substitute for analysis of our results as reported under GAAP. Some of these limitations are:

 

•
Although depreciation and amortization are non-cash charges, the assets being depreciated and amortized will often have to be replaced in the future and the Non-GAAP Measures do not reflect any cash requirements for such replacements;
•
Certain Non-GAAP Measures do not reflect our cash expenditures, or future requirements for capital expenditures or contractual commitments;
•
Certain Non-GAAP Measures do not reflect changes in, or cash requirements for, our working capital needs;
•
Certain Non-GAAP Measures do not reflect our tax expense or any cash requirements to pay income taxes;
•
Certain Non-GAAP Measures do not reflect interest expense, or the cash requirements necessary to service interest payments on our indebtedness; and
•
Certain Non-GAAP Measures do not reflect the impact of earnings or charges resulting from matters we do not consider to be indicative of our core and/or ongoing operations, but may nonetheless have a material impact on our results of operations.

 

In addition, because not all companies use identical calculations, our presentation of the Non-GAAP Measures may not be comparable to similarly titled measures of other companies, including companies in our industry.
