Lithium Americas Reports Third Quarter 2026 Results

(All amounts in US$ unless otherwise indicated)

November 12, 2026 – Vancouver, Canada: Lithium Americas Corp. (TSX: LAC) (NYSE: LAC) (“Lithium Americas,” “LAC” or the “Company”) announced that it has filed its Quarterly Report on Form 10-Q, which includes the Company’s unaudited condensed consolidated interim financial statements for the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026 (“Q3 2026”). The Company continues to progress major construction at its Thacker Pass lithium project in Humboldt County, Nevada (“Thacker Pass” or the “Project”), with Phase 11 targeting mechanical completion in late 2027, with ramp-up through 2028.

Jonathan Evans, President and Chief Executive Officer of Lithium Americas, said, “Thacker Pass is one of the largest construction projects in Nevada and is on track to deliver America's largest fully integrated source of domestic battery-quality lithium carbonate. Thacker Pass represents more than a strategic supply chain asset, it is an investment in Nevada workers and local communities, creating meaningful, family-supporting jobs. We are proud to build a lithium operation expected to help secure America’s critical mineral independence, which is key for national security.”

CAPITAL EXPENDITURE AND 2026 CAPEX GUIDANCE

The Company has completed an updated Capex Estimate for Thacker Pass Phase 1 that reflects achieving advanced levels of construction, detailed engineering and procurement and further incorporates external factors, which have arisen following declaration of the final investment decision (“FID”) in April 2025. The updated Capex Estimate of $3.83 billion (including contingency) represents an increase from the prior Capex Estimate of $2.93 billion and reflects:

•
Tariff and logistics – total impact of approximately $100 million. The tariff exposure estimate is now reduced to approximately $60 million (from the previously disclosed estimate of between $80 million and $100 million), now that most of the equipment and materials have been received. Logistics costs have been heavily impacted by increases in fuel prices, reductions in the availability of U.S. fabrication capacity and challenging global and national shipping conditions (i.e., requirements to reroute shipments due to regional constraints to protect the construction schedule, constraints in U.S. logistics and reduced open sea lane availability).
•
Craft labor wage rates and compensation package inflation – total impact of approximately $150 million. Higher than anticipated escalation in union labor craft wage rates and incentives, due to higher than anticipated levels of regional competition for skilled union labor.
•
Labor install rates for piping, steel and equipment – total impact of approximately $245 million. Labor hour costs related to install rates have been updated to reflect anticipated hours based on the current availability of quality labor. Regional competition is stretching the availability of union labor beyond what was anticipated. Actual install rates for the steel, piping and mechanical disciplines are now better known based on early installation quantities, allowing the Company to develop estimates with a higher level of accuracy and confidence.
•
Engineering, procurement and construction management (“EPCM”) and contractor staffing levels – total impact of approximately $100 million. The Project’s EPCM contractor, Bechtel, and its contractors required more engineers and construction staff to complete the Project. Given the advanced levels of detailed engineering, the required number of personnel can now be estimated with higher confidence.
•
EPCM and contractor staff wage rates – total impact of approximately $150 million. The Project’s EPCM and various contractors have also been exposed to inflationary and competitive wages, as well as increased incentives, to be able to continue to attract quality craft professionals.
•
Additional contingency – impact of approximately $150 million. Allocating an additional 10% contingency on estimated capital costs that have not yet been committed or spent.

The Capex Estimate risk profile has been lowered with advanced levels of engineering and procurement completed, all earthworks and geotechnical risks have passed, and all remaining key long-lead equipment is expected to mobilize to site during November 2026. Further, the number of craft workers already on site lowers the risk associated with being able to achieve peak labor requirements by the end of 2026. For the remainder of construction, primary risks include costs associated with attraction and retention of quality craft labor and sustaining planned installation rates.

The Company estimates the incremental funding requirement arising from the Capex Estimate and other assumption changes to be approximately $995 million, summarized as follows:

•
Incremental capital expenditure of $895 million;
•
An additional $58 million from net increases to Sustaining Capex Reserve Account requirements under the DOE Loan through March 2028, driven by increased levels of sustaining capital in 2028 and 2029; and
•
A further net increase of $42 million across all other areas, including increases to other pre-completion expenditures, partially offset by improved estimated operating cash flow during the 2028 ramp-up period, largely resulting from changes in the pricing forecast for lithium carbonate.

Pursuant to (a) an omnibus waiver, consent, and amendment to the DOE Loan (the “OWCA 3”) signed on November 10, 2026 and related loan documents, and (b) agreements among DOE, the Company, and GM, it is anticipated the incremental funding requirement will be fully met through, collectively, $650 million in contributions from the

ABOUT LITHIUM AMERICAS

Lithium Americas is building Thacker Pass, located in Humboldt County, in northern Nevada. Phase 1 is designed for nominal production capacity of 40,000 tonnes per year of battery-quality lithium carbonate, and mechanical completion is targeted for late 2027. Thacker Pass hosts the largest known measured lithium resource (Measured and Indicated) in the world and is owned by a JV between Lithium Americas and GM. The DOE holds LAC Warrants to purchase common shares equivalent to a 5% equity stake of the Company as of the Issuance Date, as well as JV Warrants to purchase a non-voting, non-transferable equity interest in the JV equivalent to a 5% interest as of the Issuance Date. Lithium Americas’ shares are listed on the Toronto Stock Exchange and New York Stock Exchange under the symbol LAC. To learn more, visit www.lithiumamericas.com or follow @LithiumAmericas on social media.
